His story

A life well lived

1947BornLouis Chukwukadibia Akah was born on 18 October 1947 in Amawbia, Anambra State, the second of six children of Mazi Akah Nwankwo, a palm-produce trader, and Madam Nwakaego Akah.

1960ScholarshipHe began school at St. Joseph's Primary School, Amawbia, walking four miles each morning with a slate under his arm. In 1960 he won a scholarship to Christ the King College, Onitsha, where he found two loves that never left him: mathematics and the school choir.

1967War yearsThe civil war closed the universities in 1967, and he spent those years teaching younger children in a refugee camp school.

1975GraduatesWhen the war ended he went to the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, and graduated in Mathematics in 1975. He always said the camp school taught him more than the university did.

1976MarriageIn 1976 he married Ngozi Eze of Nimo. Together they raised five children in a house always full of visitors, Highlife records and arguments about football.

1990PrincipalHe taught for over thirty years, and from 1990 to 2007 he was Principal of Community Secondary School, Amawbia. He knew every student by name and stood at the gate each morning in a white shirt. His favourite line is the one every former student still repeats: “Show your working.”

2003KnightA church warden at St. Joseph's, he was invested a Knight of St. Mulumba in 2003.

2008Obunakueze IIn 2008 his people conferred on him the title Obunakueze I of Umueze Village, Amawbia.

2026Called homeHe passed on peacefully at home on 10 August 2026, surrounded by family, aged 79.

Tributes

In their words

From his wife

When we married in 1976 you promised me a house of laughter. You kept that promise through lean years and full ones. You woke before the cock every morning, you prayed, and then you put the kettle on for me.

You taught our children to read the newspaper aloud and to look a person in the eye. The compound is too quiet without your radio. Rest, my dear. I will see you again.

NgoziLolo Ngozi Akah, his wife
From his children

You were the first person we called with good news and the last to hear the bad, because you always knew already. You checked our homework at the dining table with a red pen and a straight face, and you made every one of us show our working.

When Emeka failed his first JAMB, you did not shout. You sat with him on the veranda until midnight, writing out a timetable on the back of a calendar, and the next year you woke him at five every morning with tea. When Ifeoma wanted to study medicine, you sold the Peugeot 504 without telling anyone and simply said the fees were paid.

You were strict about the small things, because you said the big things are only small things added together. Shoes were polished on Saturday night. Letters were answered within the week. Visitors were never sent away without at least a cup of water and a seat in the shade.

In your last months you still asked after every grandchild by name, and you still corrected our grammar from your bed. On your final evening you asked Chiamaka to read Psalm 23 and you said the last line with her, word for word.

Thank you for the Sunday drives, for Osadebe on the gramophone, and for loving Mummy loudly in front of us. Jee nke ọma, Daddy.

Emeka, Ifeoma, Chiamaka, Tobenna & SomtoHis children
From his grandchildren

You always had groundnuts in your pocket and a riddle waiting. You let us win at draughts until we were old enough to know you were letting us. Under the ube tree you told us the stories of Amawbia, and we will keep telling them.

Your nine grandchildrenHis grandchildren
From his students

You ran Community Secondary School with a bell, a ledger and an open door. Many of us are engineers, nurses, teachers and traders today because you refused to let us give up on mathematics, or on ourselves.

A note from the family

Thank you

The family of the late Nze Dr. Louis Chukwukadibia Akah is deeply grateful for the love that has surrounded us since 10 August.

To everyone who visited, prayed, cooked, sang, sent messages or travelled to Amawbia: thank you. To the doctors and nurses who cared for Dede in his last months, and to Rev. Fr. Anselm Okeke and the parish of St. Joseph's, we are grateful beyond words.

May God reward your kindness, and may you return safely to your homes.
